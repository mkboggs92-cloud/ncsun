The ride so far

Each season was predicted by models trained only on earlier seasons, using what was known at 5am on game day. Spreads and totals share one card: at most 6 picks a weekday and 12 on Saturday, one bet per game, graded at the best 5am price at DraftKings, Caesars or FanDuel. Simulated results, not a live record.

The theory

Fade the luck. Respect the air.

Shooting in small samples is mostly noise, but lines are built partly from results. When a team's shot-making runs far ahead of what its shots should produce, it gets overrated, and the model bets against it. Totals add one quirk the market barely prices: altitude.

Zone luck

Actual minus expected points at the rim, from midrange and from three, on offense and defense, season to date. Expected rates shrink toward last season's league average, so a few hot nights barely move it.

KenPom drift

A team's KenPom rating this morning minus its preseason rating. Teams that have jumped furthest since preseason tend to be overrated, so the model fades them too.

Ensembles

Five machine-learning models for spreads (31 variables, each game fed both ways to remove home bias) and five for totals (68 variables: pace, efficiency, luck, KenPom drift, preseason projections, altitude and more). Each votes on a side and a strength.

Altitude

Teams at 5,000 ft and up post inflated scoring at home, and totals get set off those averages. The model bets overs at high venues and unders when those teams come down to sea level, when the totals ensemble agrees. The edge grows with elevation.

The card

Ranked: totals altitude, spread tri-agree, then spread luck + ensemble. Every pick is 1 unit: the tiers have similar measured edges, so flat staking keeps it simple. Totals tri-agree is tracked on paper, not bet. At most 6 picks a weekday and 12 on Saturday, one bet per game, and only picks with a line at DK, Caesars or FanDuel. When a spread and an altitude total collide, the altitude total wins.

What it doesn't bet

  • Any game where either team has played fewer than 5 games. Every early-season model and rule tested lost money at real prices.
  • Neutral-site games, for the luck tiers (altitude totals can play at neutral venues).
  • Ensemble-only spreads and totals with no luck or altitude signal. They don't beat these books.
  • Two bets on one game.

How the backtest stayed honest

  • Walk-forward: every season predicted only from earlier seasons.
  • Every stat from games before tip-off; KenPom and Barttorvik snapshots dated before each game.
  • Graded at the real 5am price and juice, not -110.
  • Random sides win about 51% at these prices. With the models retrained on shuffled results the card still hits about 54%, so the luck and altitude rules carry most of the edge and the ensemble adds roughly 3 points on top.
  • Picks beat the closing number in every season. Graded at the near-close consensus line the card still wins 55.7%; at the plain 5am consensus and -110, 56.9%.
  • Two full audits regraded every pick from the raw odds feed and checked every score against ESPN: zero mismatches. The second one found that 20% of games had never been matched to a line (team-name and home/away quirks in the odds feed); they are now in.

What to expect live

At 1 unit a pick the backtest went 57.1% over 2,453 bets, +216 units, +8.8% ROI, with a worst drawdown of 19 units. Tiers, cutoffs and caps were chosen on the same seasons they were tested on, so expect less live: roughly 55% and +4 to +5% ROI, about +25 to +30 units over a 600-bet season, with about a 1-in-6 chance of a losing season. Variance is real: even with a true 57% edge, 100-bet stretches at 50% happen. The live tab is the record that counts.

Under the hood · model specification

The engine room

Every equation, loss function and hyperparameter behind the card. Everything is computed point-in-time: a game's inputs use only information timestamped before 5:00 a.m. ET on game day.

01 · Point-in-time features

Season-to-date team statistics are cumulative sums with the current game removed, so game g never sees its own box score.

St(g)=∑j=1g−1st,j(cumsum − current)

KenPom ratings come from the dated daily archive; Barttorvik from its time-machine snapshots. League baselines come from the prior season only. Both teams need at least 5 prior games.

02 · Bayesian-shrunk zone luck

For zone z ∈ {rim, mid, three} with m makes on a attempts, expected rate shrinks toward the prior-season league rate with pseudo-count kz:

p^z=mz+kzpzlgaz+kzΛoff=∑zvzaz(mzaz−p^z)

vz = point value (2, 2, 3). Offense k = 100 / 150 / 250; defense k = 200 / 400 / 1,500. Net luck = Λoff − Λdef per game; the game signal is home minus away. Tails are fixed at the 19th / 81st percentiles of a held-out season.

03 · Rating drift

Dt=AdjEMttoday−AdjEMtpreDgame=Dhome−Daway

Faded at the 15% tails. For totals, the analogue is scoring-environment drift (offense + defense efficiency vs preseason) and KenPom's preseason projected total vs the line.

04 · Mirror augmentation (spreads)

Let P swap home and away and negate every directional feature. Training doubles to {(x, y), (Px, −y)}, and predictions are made antisymmetric so the model cannot learn a phantom home bias:

fsym(x)=12[f(x)−f(Px)]⇒fsym(Px)=−fsym(x)

Target: cover margin y = (home − away) + spread at 5am. Totals target: (home + away) − total at 5am.

08 · Decision rule and card

bet⇔sgn(E)=sgn(−Λ)∧|E|≥τtierstake∈{1u}

The signal is the faded luck sign −Λ (plus drift or preseason-total agreement for tri-agree tiers, τ = 1 SD for totals) or the altitude sign A ∈ {+1 over at 5,000 ft+, −1 under for 5,000 ft+ teams below 2,000 ft}, where only the sign of E must agree.

Tiers rank as totals altitude › spread tri-agree › spread luck + ensemble; totals tri-agree is tracked on paper only. Picks with no line at DK, Caesars or FanDuel are dropped before the cap. Per day the top K are kept, K = 6 (weekdays) or 12 (Saturday), subject to one bet per game.
